What this app does

You drop your bank statement PDFs into a Google Drive folder. The app reads them, works out the bank, currency, and every transaction line, and files each one under a category — automatically, using your past corrections as precedent. From there you get a searchable transaction list, spending dashboards converted to a single currency, and a place to ask plain-language questions like “how much did I spend on dining last month?”

Every night, it also writes a full CSV backup of your transactions to a separate Drive folder and keeps the most recent 7 — quietly, with no action needed from you.

01 · First run

Getting started

Three things happen once, in order, before the app has anything to show you.

  1. Sign in Use the username and password you were given. The app is single-user — there's no sign-up screen.
  2. Connect Google Drive Open Settings and click Connect Google Drive. You'll be sent to Google's own consent screen — approving it is what lets the app read your statement folder and write nightly backups.
  3. Run your first ingestion Put a few statement PDFs in the connected Drive folder, then go to Ingestion and click Run Ingestion. Come back to the Dashboard once it finishes.

02 · Overview

Dashboard

Four views of the same date range: what you spent by category, income against expenses, how spending splits across banks, and what recurring payments are due. Set the From / To fields once and the first three follow.

  • Category Trends — a grouped bar per month, one color per category, so a spike in any one area is easy to spot.
  • Cash Flow — income versus expenses side by side, month over month.
  • Bank Breakdown — the same spending split out by which bank account it came from.
  • Recurring Payments — every subscription and bill you're tracking, with a due/paid/overdue status per cycle. Once a payment is trusted, a matching charge auto-applies; anything less certain waits here for your review.
Currency

Everything is converted to a single reporting currency automatically. When an exact exchange rate for a transaction's date wasn't available, the app uses the nearest one it has and says so in a small notice above the chart — nothing is hidden or silently estimated.

03 · The full ledger

Transactions

Every transaction the app has ever extracted, searchable and filterable, with the converted amount always shown alongside the original.

  • Search by description, or filter by bank, category, or currency.
  • The UNSURE only shortcut jumps straight to anything the app couldn't confidently categorize.
  • Click a transaction's category to correct it — the app remembers the correction and uses it as precedent for similar transactions from then on.
  • Export CSV exports exactly the rows your current filters and search are showing.

04 · Plain-language questions

Ask AI

Ask a question the way you'd ask a person, and get an answer grounded in your own transaction data for the date range you choose — not general financial advice.

  • Set a From / To range, or tick Use all transactions to skip the date filter entirely.
  • Every answer opens with a short disclaimer: it's an observation about your own numbers, not financial guidance.
  • Each row on the Transactions page also has its own Ask AI shortcut, for a question scoped to just that one transaction.

05 · Turning PDFs into data

Ingestion

This is the only screen where new statements actually become transactions. Everything else in the app is reading from what Ingestion has already processed.

  • Click Run Ingestion to scan the connected Drive folder for PDFs it hasn't seen before — already-processed statements are detected and skipped automatically, so it's always safe to run again.
  • A run in progress can be cancelled from this page at any time.
  • Run History keeps every past run, with how many files were found, processed, skipped, and failed.
  • A statement that fails (usually a scan quality issue) is marked failed with a reason — it never blocks the rest of that run.

06 · What needs your attention

Review

Three independent things live on this page: category corrections waiting on your judgment, transactions where two categorization methods disagreed, and the status of last night's backup.

  • When you correct a transaction's category, the app looks for similar past transactions. A near-certain match is applied automatically; anything less certain is listed here for you to Approve or Reject — one at a time or selected in bulk.
  • Rejecting a proposal never changes the transaction — it's left exactly as it was.
  • Category Disagreements — sometimes the fuzzy-match and the AI classifier land on two different categories for the same new transaction. Rather than silently pick one, the app shows both and asks you to choose.
Backup Status

A full CSV snapshot of every transaction is written to a dedicated Drive backup folder every night, with the 7 most recent kept and older ones cleaned up automatically. This panel shows when that last happened. If it ever asks you to reconnect Google Drive, head to Settings — the connection can occasionally need re-approving.

07 · Connections and categories

Settings

Everything that's configured once and rarely touched again — plus, further down, every tunable value the app runs on.

  • Google Drive shows whether the app is connected, and a button to connect or reconnect. Reconnecting doesn't lose anything — it's the same one-time consent step as the first time.
  • Categories is your whitelist — add, rename, or remove any category. UNSURE is reserved by the app itself and can't be removed; it's where anything it couldn't confidently place ends up.
Removing a category

A category already used by existing transactions can't be deleted outright — the app will tell you how many transactions are affected so you can recategorize them first.

Application Settings

Below the categories list, grouped by area (matching, embeddings, recurring payments, backups, and more) — every value the app runs on, with a plain-language description of what it does. Editing one shows exactly which container needs restarting, and every past change is kept in a history log.

Sensible defaults

Nothing here needs to be touched to use the app day to day — the defaults are what a real deployment runs on. This exists for the moment you do want to tune something, not as a checklist to work through.
